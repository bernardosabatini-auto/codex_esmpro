# Rotation verdict and inherited-frame correction

ProteinAE's measured latent representation is rotation-dependent: sixteen identical training structures under eight rigid poses gave latent RMS difference 0.98927. Applying the same deterministic N/CA/C frame before encoding reduced it to 0.00000198. Translation was already removed to numerical precision. This is a representation property; it does not by itself prove that rotation explains the predictor's accuracy limitation.

## Correction to the previous interpretation

The original cache was **already PCA-canonicalized**. Read-only inspection of `esm_proae/code/gate6_100k.py` and `gate8_build_afdb.py` shows `canonicalize_pyg_data` before encoding. `code/canonicalize.py` uses CA PCA axes, skewness-based signs and a right-handed determinant correction. This existing preprocessing was missed when interpreting fresh raw-reference labels as compatible with the inherited head.

Consequently, both the fresh raw-file labels and the new first-residue-frame labels differ from the inherited cache convention. The previous four-arm pilot remains a valid comparison of those label recipes, but cannot establish that the inherited pipeline had untreated arbitrary rotations. It also does not show that canonicalization in general is ineffective. Its first-residue convention was a change of convention.

The initial reference-alignment profile (49651393) preserves geometry and reconstruction, but raw-file reference latents still differ from inherited cached latents by mean RMS0.97910 on128 training proteins. Thus raw-file alignment must not be promoted as an inherited-compatible fix. After alignment to the fixed raw-file reference, externally rotated teacher inputs recover the same encoding (worst tested latent RMS difference0.00000962), confirming the alignment mechanism independently of cache compatibility.

## Corrected experiment

Recover the original reference frame directly from the inherited, sequence-mapped `ca_coords`; rigidly place the verified full reference backbone into that frame. Preserve cached reference `z` values bit-for-bit. Fit one proper global transform per teacher conformation to a shared confident core of that fixed reference, then apply it to every atom before encoding. Insufficient or degenerate cores use the predeclared all-CA fallback. No sample filtering, domain-wise fitting, coordinate averaging or latent averaging is introduced.

First verify fresh re-encoding against cached latent targets, along with all-sample reconstruction and pose controls. Then compare cached-reference-only training with cached-reference plus aligned teacher supervision using matched seeds, data, budgets and both predeclared checkpoints. No reference structure is required at inference.

Independent PCA canonicalization of each conformation can change axes/sign choices when the structure changes, particularly near eigenvalue or sign degeneracy. Aligning conformers to one fixed per-sequence reference avoids that particular change of convention. Whether this improves state coverage remains an experimental question.

For a future autoencoder redesign, explicitly enforce rotation-invariant latent codes, with reconstruction assessed up to proper rigid alignment and output pose handled separately. Ordinary rotation augmentation alone does not guarantee invariant latents. For the current pretrained model, preserve its existing convention rather than replacing it silently.

## Why not independently PCA-align every conformation?

Across all 8,192 already reference-aligned teacher samples, independently applying the inherited PCA/skewness convention adds a median 43.1° frame change; 39.5% exceed 90°. Even among the 1,176 samples within 1 Å aligned-core RMSD of their reference, 17.0% exceed 90°. Only 2.53% of teacher samples have near-degenerate PCA eigenvalues. These angles combine continuous axis movement and sign/axis changes; they do not establish a causal accuracy loss. The [full diagnostic](pca_frame_diagnostic_20261001.md) supports fixing one frame per sequence instead of recomputing it for each conformation. This prevents within-ensemble frame changes; it does not make the encoder invariant or remove possible frame discontinuities across sequences.

## Final experimental verdict

The corrected preparation passes rigid-pose controls (worst tested cached-frame latent RMSE 0.0000131), bitwise identity checks on all 512 reference latent arrays, and the full 8,192-conformation reconstruction audit. Apply one proper global rigid transform in coordinate space **before** encoding; an arbitrary eight-dimensional latent code cannot be rotated with the coordinate-space 3×3 matrix. Do not align domains independently, since that would remove real relative domain motion.

The matched training and both checkpoint ensembles are complete. At 2,000 updates, aligned-teacher supervision yields native CA-lDDT 0.78758 versus initial 0.78384, with an improvement interval spanning zero. Final ensemble state coverage is 0.43750, identical to initialization and below the reference-only control's 0.46875. Ensemble nearest-reference quality declines from 0.86794 to 0.86461 and fails the predeclared noninferiority screen. MD distribution distance improves, but no primary diversity gain qualifies. Keep original weights; see the [completed campaign](cached_frame_reflow_campaign_20261001.md).

The representation-level conclusion is strong: rotation can dominate latent differences, and using one fixed reference frame prevents nuisance pose changes within a sequence's ensemble. The modeling conclusion is limited: this intervention has not established improved accuracy or useful state coverage. The matched comparison tests aligned-teacher supervision against reference-only supervision, not alignment alone; a causal alignment test would require the same teacher conformations independently PCA-encoded as the additional control. Fixed-reference preprocessing also leaves cross-sequence frame discontinuities unresolved. An intrinsically invariant encoder would require an invariant architecture and pose-aware or alignment-invariant reconstruction training. This is distinct from wrapping the existing encoder with a deterministic canonicalizer, which can already make the composite mapping invariant under rigid input rotations without retraining ProteinAE.

## Encoder mechanism and invariance qualification

Read-only inspection of `ProteinAE_v1/proteinfoundation/nn/protein_transformer.py` shows coordinate components entering learned linear layers directly: the backbone atom encoder adds `linear_3d_embed(coors_3d)` at lines696–699; the CA-only branch does likewise at lines1180–1182. The output is then projected into eight channels and layer-normalized (lines956–960). These are ordinary learned features, not an explicitly constrained rotation representation. This matches the observed rotation sensitivity; it does not prove every learned feature carries pose.

The earlier wording that truly invariant codes necessarily require retraining the encoder was too strong. If a deterministic canonicalization function C satisfies C(Rx+t)=C(x), then the composite E(C(x)) is rotation-invariant even when E itself is not. Our fixed N/CA/C frame control demonstrates this to numerical precision for complete nondegenerate backbones. PCA also provides canonicalization away from ambiguities, but its frame can change abruptly as the shape changes. Choosing a different canonicalizer changes target codes for the pretrained prediction head; that compatibility issue is separate from whether ProteinAE itself must be retrained. No change to the original encoder code was made.

## Matched frame-only capacity test at 2,000 updates

The corrected reliable32 experiment now compares the same16 teacher conformations per protein in fixed-reference and independently PCA-encoded frames, with matched initialization, target batches, label draws and optimization. At primary CFG1, final recall is0.62500 aligned versus0.66369 PCA. The paired family interval for aligned minus PCA is[-0.10595,0.03333]; the early500-update aligned advantage does not persist. Both improve teacher fidelity and fail the capacity criterion versus initialization. Thus nuisance pose changes are real, but fixed-reference alignment has not demonstrated a final learning advantage in this test. This narrows the earlier mechanism-based preference; it does not invalidate canonicalization or justify ignoring latent pose. See [final comparison](overfit_comparison_2000.md).

## Balanced-prior frame test and current conclusion

The matched frame comparison has now also finished with equal-state teacher sampling. At2,000 updates, aligned recall is0.89360 and PCA recall0.85610; aligned minus PCA is0.03750 with paired family95% interval[-0.01637,0.09970]. The interval includes zero. Aligned passes the capacity gate while PCA narrowly fails its initialization comparison, but that pass/fail distinction is not evidence that the frames differ. Both frames improve over their empirical-prior counterparts: aligned recall increases0.26860[0.18333,0.35491], PCA0.19241[0.09345,0.28854]. The supported training intervention is the teacher-state prior. See the [balanced final comparison](overfit_balanced_comparison_2000.md).

Separate tuning favors some aligned checkpoints, but it does not turn the unresolved frame contrast into a demonstrated rotation benefit. The aligned500 model preserves aggregate experimental-state coverage, and the aligned2000 model loses coverage despite improving nearest-reference quality:0.40625 versus0.43750 for the original, difference-0.03125[-0.09375,0]. Neither establishes an external diversity gain; see the [final ensemble comparison](balanced2000_ensemble_comparison.md). All these are development results, with the original34 and reserved17 tests still unscored.

The practical recommendation remains: preserve the inherited canonical convention, apply one proper global coordinate transform before encoding every conformation in a sequence's ensemble, and validate pose invariance and reconstruction numerically. Never apply a3×3 coordinate rotation to the eight learned latent channels. Deterministic canonicalization can make the composite encoder invariant to input rigid pose; it does not guarantee smooth codes across changing shapes or remove uncertainty near frame degeneracies. Fixed-reference alignment is a consistent label recipe, with no demonstrated final superiority over independent PCA in the matched capacity tests. Do not attribute current gains from balanced sampling, reduced guidance or conditioning reuse to a rotation fix.
