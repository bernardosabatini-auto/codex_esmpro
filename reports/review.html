A promising predictor buried under unreliable conclusions

Initial review: 29 September 2026; the audit below describes that initial snapshot. Original code/data remain read only. Subsequent execution is authorized for up to eight concurrent H200 requests, scoped strictly to registered project jobs.

Verdict: keep the sequence-to-latent predictor and frozen decoder. The existing results support a useful fast-prediction research direction. They do not establish equal-compute accuracy, novel folds, a fundamental 0.59 ceiling, or a validated design platform. The project has expanded faster than its measurement system can support. Stop adding branches until a small, reproducible benchmark establishes what actually works.

The core question should again be concrete: Can we produce a usable single predicted structure from sequence, at a reproducible accuracy–latency advantage over a strong baseline? Candidate sets are also a legitimate output, but require their own utility and selection criteria. A true-reference oracle is not an inference algorithm.

Execution update: 30 September 2026

The new frozen-head comparison, selective-precision checks, and fresh ESMFold2-Fast benchmark now use fixed-correspondence scoring with complete coverage. Mean TM is 0.56824 for the pair head and 0.59977 for ESMFold2-Fast. These values supersede the legacy alignment scores for current decisions. Nine models were trained in bounded, matched pilots. The structural auxiliary recipe and source-confidence residue weighting both failed the promotion rule; retain the untouched checkpoint. Only two targets exceed 256 residues, so it provides weak coverage for long-chain claims.

Data and evaluation are the first scientific problem

214/626 experimental targets have a Cα separation above 4.5 Å between stored adjacent residues: 394 such gaps in total. None of the 626 records has a stored original residue-index map. These are direct CPU measurements of the HDF5 coordinates, not model predictions.

gate15_build_pdb.py keeps observed residues with complete backbone atoms, concatenates their sequence, and stores a chain-break count. It discards original numbering. That can turn an experimentally incomplete protein into a different sequence for ESMC and asks the model to treat distant observed residues as adjacent. This complicates both folding and bond-length evaluation. It does not prove the accuracy gap is caused by gaps, since both methods receive the same input; it makes the benchmark definition weaker than the report suggests.

The separation filter is useful but overstated. gate27_select_exp_val.py uses a heuristic MMseqs search, an E-value threshold and at least 50% coverage on either side. It skips missing training files and missing sequence attributes. That establishes “no detected qualifying match in the supplied files,” not a guarantee that every sequence shares less than 30% identity with every relevant training sequence. It also does not establish independence from the frozen ESMC, ProteinAE or comparator training sets. Train/test identity and family labels need explicit provenance.

Comparing CAMEO and exp-val means does not causally isolate the effect of identity filtering: the datasets also differ in length, folds, completeness and other selection rules. Repeated model selection on exp-val has now made it development data. Reserve a fresh final test, including an explicit release-date policy and documented pretraining-overlap limitations.

For sequence-to-structure prediction, retain residue correspondence. The existing Foldseek helper uses structure alignment, allowing residues to be rematched, and takes the larger of query- and target-normalized scores. That can be useful for fold similarity, but is not interchangeable with evaluation at the expected residue correspondence. Use fixed-correspondence TM, Cα lDDT, RMSD and geometry checks; retain free structural alignment only as a secondary diagnostic. US-align's official implementation supports residue-constrained comparisons through -TMscore/-byresi. Do not call a TM-shaped score after Kabsch “optimized TM”: Kabsch minimizes squared distances, a different objective.

The code makes silent errors too easy

  • Import-time behavior and mutable globals: gate7 changes directories and imports a large trainer; gate10 monkey-patches gate7; many controls come from environment variables. The effective experiment cannot be inferred from one configuration.
  • Incomplete randomness control: flow seeds are batch-order dependent; the decoder samples from the global RNG; current RePaint code still uses torch.randn_like(x) outside the supplied generator. A seeded main noise draw is not a fully seeded experiment.
  • Inconsistent failure denominators: gate7 means omit missing scores while best-of-K can impute zero; gate31 aggregates only backbones with scored results. Coverage warnings are insufficient for headline aggregation. Existing headline rows happen to report full coverage; the mechanism is still fragile.
  • Silent truncation and selection loss: the HDF5 reader truncates to an environment-dependent maximum; named-target selection silently drops IDs not present in the dataset. This is particularly dangerous for long-chain comparisons.
  • Weak report provenance: gate33 advertises reading results but hardcodes accuracy, ablation and contact curves. It maps a noise-interpolation label to gate28/uncond, and merges result dictionaries with filename-pattern matching. The report should be generated from an explicit table of immutable records.
  • Expensive static work repeated during sampling: the pair representation is cached, but conditional projections and all per-layer pair-bias projections are rebuilt on every trunk call. This is a concrete optimization to profile and validate before enlarging models.

These are not reasons to discard all previous work. They are reasons to stop treating plausible curves as evidence until the experiment contract is executable. Printing settings is helpful; tests that demonstrate their effects are stronger.

Proposed accuracy promotion rule: at least +0.01 paired TM on locked development targets, cluster-bootstrap lower bound above zero, replication across three training seeds, and no material lDDT or geometry loss. These are practical thresholds to decide where to spend resources, not significance claims about existing runs. The fresh final test is evaluated once after choices are fixed.

My priority order is measurement and input integrity → cost-matched baselines → representation/conditioning diagnostics → a small number of controlled improvements. I would not start another 840M run, another generative steering sweep, or ESMC fine-tuning now. Earlier-layer work is deferred; it will only reopen if a specific new finding warrants it. Repeating negative layer searches is not a current priority.

Good reconstruction plus poor latent interpolation specifically argues for studying the representation's local geometry. Possible responses include decoder-sensitive weighting or controlled pose augmentation with re-encoding; both need small pilots because they can also make training worse. I would not immediately replace ProteinAE or invent another large model. First identify whether the dominant error is missing sequence information, target ambiguity, poor optimization or an unstable decoder neighborhood.

What is implemented in the new folder

src/latentfold contains the extracted pair and pair-free architectures, strict CPU checkpoint loading, an explicit differentiable decoder adapter, flow loss, deterministic sampler, target-specific noise streams, strict data reads, and paired/geometry metrics. Defaults do not choose CUDA automatically. The copied model classes preserve state-dict names; they remove environment controls, auto-compilation and monkey-patching.

scripts/audit_existing.py reproduces this numerical audit without loading a model. scripts/predict.py provides inference from cached embeddings into full-backbone coordinates, with per-target/sample records, explicit decoder noise and a run manifest. It refuses existing output directories and records interrupted runs as failures. This is a research entry point, not a claim of a new trained predictor.

Ten CPU tests pass. The small extracted architectures match isolated legacy forward computations exactly, with nonzero randomized weights. A real pretrained ProteinAE CPU check reconstructed target 1p65_A (57 residues) with 0.113 Å Cα RMSD and deterministic repeated decoding. This verifies the adapter on one target; it does not validate full-scale inference, every checkpoint or GPU performance. The external US-align executable was unavailable on PATH, so only its strict wrapper/parser has been tested.

Large data, pretrained weights and the modified ProteinAE checkout were not copied. The original tree was left unchanged. There is no distributed trainer. Independent single-GPU training, explicit job registration, a project-wide GPU cap and a persistent completion-analysis watcher are now implemented.
